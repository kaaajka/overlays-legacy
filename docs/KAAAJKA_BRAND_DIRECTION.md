# Kaaajka visual language

Authority: the supplied peach/orange channel banner, controller-holding avatar with pink headphones and floral dark hoodie, and nineteen expressive white bunny emotes. References are archived unchanged under `docs/assets/kaaajka-brand/`; the manifest records source names, hashes and animation metadata.

Warm peach, blush pink and soft orange are welcoming accents, not mandatory backgrounds. White carries bunny/type highlights; occasional cyan signals a contrasting reaction. Dark outlines and pale interior shapes preserve the small sticker silhouette. Keep source-specific GIF colors intact.

Use rounded sticker edges, short overshoots, squashed landings and expressive pauses where a subject's joke supports them. A pair of ears can mark a community response; a recognizable original emote can answer a hand-heart or absurd moment. Do not scatter mascots as confetti or cover donor data. Preserve their honest source resolution.

Poppins remains the established overlay voice. Donor names and amounts keep separate readable space, a warm pale fill and a restrained dark outline. Studio uses compact sans labels and genuine monospace timecode in a graphite shell, with peach selection and cyan secondary measurement.

Each show keeps its own composition and musical gesture. Brand lives in the friendly reaction, sticker handling and authored gratitude, not seven pink skins. Cash is a celebratory payoff on selected phrases; it should thin before an impact and settle afterward.

## Stage and takeover

1920×1080 provides placement space. Localized shows preserve gameplay. Lower tiers use roughly the central 25–40% of available area; middle tiers 40–55%; top-tier cash/interruption can briefly expand. Percentages describe intent, not a quota or opaque-screen coverage. Large takeovers require a named cue, limited duration and a readable return.

Use no bunny decoration in deadpan bathroom footage when it weakens the joke. Keep the masked dancer's red/white silhouette, the rodent's coarse film-strip rhythm and the webcam's pixel character. The banner gradient is a reference for warmth, not a full-frame overlay background. Emote imagery belongs only to meaningful reactions and a small Studio identity mark.
